# Question-shaping guide — lessons learned

An evolving guide to preparing and reviewing learning questions. Started on 25 October 2026 following the aids-to-navigation experiment.

These preferences update the provisional approach in the README wherever they differ.

## 1. Do not ask for confidence levels

Do not ask me to rate my confidence, either for individual answers or for a session. Keep answering questions simple and low-friction.

## 2. Do not record outcomes

Do not maintain assessment histories, outcome records, mastery ratings or performance tracking. I can judge my progress over time from the typical scores I am getting.

Giving a score and useful feedback during a review is fine; it does not need to become a persistent record. This guide records lessons about question design, not my assessment results.

## 3. Make multiple-choice alternatives unambiguous

Check that each alternative communicates its intended meaning clearly and that only one is defensible under the question's stated assumptions. Avoid phrasing that makes interpreting the alternatives harder than understanding the subject.

In the first experiment, the alternatives for interpreting `Fl(3) W 15s` led to a disagreement about wording despite the learner knowing the intended meaning. A clearer approach would be:

> A light is charted **Fl(3) W 15s**. Describe what you would expect to see, and explain what the fifteen seconds measures.

Use a short free-text question when it tests understanding more clearly than multiple choice. This does not require abandoning multiple choice generally.

## 4. Check interpretation before diagnosing a knowledge gap

A selected letter alone may not reveal the learner's understanding. If an answer is disputed or the wording may have caused confusion, ask how the learner interpreted it before concluding that there is a misconception.

Give the learner's explanation more weight than the selected letter, and acknowledge a question-design problem where appropriate.

## Prompt to use when returning for a review

Use the following prompt, replacing the question filename for a different session:

```text
Read /Users/pete/repos/yachtmaster-learning-aide
Then assess my answers to your questions in aids-to-navigation-experiment-01.md
```

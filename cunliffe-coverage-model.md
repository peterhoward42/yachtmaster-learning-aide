# Chapter and subsection coverage model

A provisional planning model for Tom Cunliffe’s *The Complete Yachtmaster*, 10th edition (2021). Created 25 October 2026.

## Purpose and limits

Use the book’s structure and approximate reading volume to size an initial question bank, identify what that bank samples, and expand it deliberately. This is a content inventory, not a learner performance record. It does not track scores, confidence, mastery or assessment history.

Source: [public 10th-edition preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781472982971_A42450030/preview-9781472982971_A42450030.pdf), PDF pages 6–8. Chapter and subsection starts were transcribed from the contents. See also [contents reference](cunliffe-contents-reference.md). The full chapter text has not been reviewed for this model.

Page spans measure space, not conceptual density, difficulty or importance. Illustrations, worked examples and page layout affect them. The listed subsections are the contents-page headings, not necessarily every heading inside the chapters. A single question per heading establishes only an initial sample.

## How the estimates work

1. **Chapter span:** subtract its start page from the next chapter’s start page. Chapter 32 ends at the start of Appendix 1. These are nominal spans; boundary pages may contain material from both chapters.
2. **Subsection span:** use the next distinct listed start page as the boundary. Material before the first listed subsection is a separate opening row so it is not lost.
3. **Shared starts:** when several headings begin on the same page, their combined span is known only approximately. Divide that span equally for allocation purposes. The resulting fractional **page weights are bookkeeping assumptions, not measured section lengths**. Even headings starting on different pages have uncertain within-page boundaries.
4. **First-pass chapter questions:** take the largest of **6**, **1.5 × chapter pages rounded up**, and **number of listed subsections + 2**. The density of 1.5 questions per page is an initial design choice, not an established optimum. The subsection floor prevents short, broad chapters from losing headings.
5. **Allocate within the chapter:** reserve two questions for connections or decisions spanning sections. Give each listed subsection one question (or the whole chapter one if no subsections are listed). Distribute the remaining questions in proportion to page weights, rounding by largest remainder; ties follow book order. Opening material has no guaranteed minimum, but can receive a proportional allocation. Its concepts may also be addressed by integration questions.

The total is a starting bank size, not a single sitting: use short sets of roughly 5–8 questions. The two integration questions should connect ideas rather than duplicate subsection questions. Counts can be revised after inspecting the actual material; there is no obligation to manufacture weak questions to meet a quota.

## Expanding coverage systematically

Treat the first pass as a breadth sample. Before writing questions for a section, read it and create a small list of distinct learning objectives, each linked to its section ID. Broad headings such as “Sails”, “Radar” or “Navigation in poor conditions” will need finer objectives than the contents supply.

For each objective, inventory the available questions against these dimensions:

| Dimension | What to sample |
|---|---|
| Understanding | Explain the mechanism or relationship |
| Application | Use it in a realistic sailing decision |
| Diagnosis | Recognise an error, misleading cue or failure |
| Essential recall or procedure | Retrieve a necessary fact or describe a useful sequence |
| Transfer and limits | Apply it under changed conditions and recognise when it is insufficient |

Do not force every dimension onto every objective. Use clear multiple choice where suitable and short free text where that tests understanding more cleanly. Avoid asking for confidence ratings.

Suggested expansion sequence:

1. **Breadth:** fill any missing section or learning objective. A first-pass quota can still leave several objectives unsampled.
2. **Depth:** add a different question dimension for objectives represented by only one kind of question.
3. **Variation:** add changed conditions, plausible errors and alternative scenarios, prioritising consequences and practical judgement over page length.
4. **Integration:** connect objectives across sections and chapters, with explicit links to each objective.
5. **Syllabus gaps:** map objectives to the selected official RYA syllabus and add material beyond the book where required. Book coverage alone does not establish syllabus coverage or practical competence.
